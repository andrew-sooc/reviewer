LESSON 01 • MECHANICS

🚀 Impulse and Momentum

Momentum describes how much motion an object has. It depends on its mass and velocity.

Linear Momentum

p = mv

p = momentum (kg·m/s)
m = mass (kg)
v = velocity (m/s)

Example: A 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s has a momentum of 6 kg·m/s.

💥 Impulse

I = FΔt = Δp

I = impulse (N·s)
F = net force (N)
Δt = time interval (s)

Impulse is equal to the change in momentum.

Remember: Impulse-Momentum Theorem: FΔt = mvf - mvi

⚖️ Conservation of Momentum

In an isolated system, total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after it.

p initial = p final

For two objects:

m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂'
Example: Two skaters push away from each other. Their individual momenta change, but total momentum of the system remains constant if external forces are negligible.

💫 Types of Collisions

Elastic Collision

Both momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved.

Example: An ideal collision between billiard balls.

Inelastic Collision

Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not. Some energy changes into sound, heat, or deformation.

Example: Two objects sticking together after collision.

LESSON 02 • ELECTRICITY & ELECTROMAGNETISM

⚡ Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction happens when a changing magnetic field through a conductor or coil produces an induced voltage.

Key idea: A changing magnetic field can produce electricity.

🔄 Electric Motors vs. Generators

Electric Motor

Converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Generator

Converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

🏭 Power Generation and Distribution

1. Generation

Power plants use generators to produce electricity.

2. Step-up Transformer

Increases voltage for efficient long-distance transmission.

3. Transmission Lines

Carry high-voltage electricity over long distances.

4. Step-down Transformer

Lowers voltage before electricity reaches consumers.

Safety reminder: Never touch power lines or electrical equipment.
